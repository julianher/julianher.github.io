02Overview

Government data, treated as a career asset.

Most H-1B reporting stops at "here's the volume by company." This project starts there and keeps going, pairing application volume with location-aware salary distributions, top-employer breakdowns, and percentile wage data so candidates can answer the questions that actually matter: which states pay well and sponsor often, which employers are realistic at their level, and what salary they should ask for.

The result is a two-dashboard journey: Location Strategy for the macro view and Career Strategy for the micro, split deliberately so each screen answers one question well.

03Technologies

What it's built on.

Tableau Desktop
Visual analytics, dashboard composition, and interactive parameter configuration, including dynamic "Top N" controls and cross-dashboard filter context.
Tableau Prep Builder
End-to-end ELT pipeline covering type coercion, casing normalisation, and resolution of missing geographical markers to produce a clean, visualisation-ready dataset.
AI analytics assistants
Gemini 3.0 and Claude used as thinking partners for workflow optimisation, analysis strategy, and pressure-testing dashboard structure.
04Core skills

What it demonstrates.

Data pipeline engineering
Cleaned and transformed raw CSV data by standardising string casings, converting data types, and resolving missing geographical markers to produce a visualisation-ready dataset of 83,000+ records.
Strategic dashboard UI/UX
Employed a dual-dashboard strategy to prevent cognitive overload, splitting the user journey into a distinct "Location Strategy" view and a "Career Strategy" view.
Dynamic data modelling
Built user-controlled "Top N" parameters and context filters that let viewers seamlessly toggle between top companies and top roles without breaking background calculations.
Actionable business intelligence
Translated 83,000+ records into concrete, defensible recommendations for salary negotiation and targeted job hunting.
05Deep dive · features & insights

Functional storytelling, four ways.

001/04

The geographic landscape. Where to go.

A spatial heatmap visualises the density of H-1B filings across the United States. Rather than tracking volume in isolation, the dashboard pairs each state with location-specific salary metrics, letting candidates balance job availability against cost of living and earning potential in the same view.

002/04

The career navigator. Who to work for.

Scatter plots and treemaps break down the reality of corporate sponsorship, including the long tail. Amazon may top the chart, but it represents only about 3% of total filings, which reframes the realistic field of targets well beyond the household-name employers.

003/04

Data-driven negotiation. What to ask for.

Averages mislead when it comes to pay. Box-and-whisker plots replace them with 25th to 75th percentile salary ranges for specific roles like Data Scientists and Software Developers across individual states, giving candidates a defensible anchor for offer conversations.

004/04

Real-time market pulse. At a glance.

Custom KPIs anchor the dashboard the moment it loads: 83,064 applications in the quarter, a 7-day median processing time, and a heavy 62.5% concentration in technical roles, so the user knows what they're walking into before drilling down.
